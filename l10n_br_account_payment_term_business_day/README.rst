Brazilian taxes are not due on a day the banks do not settle, and the
rule differs per tax: some anticipate to the previous banking business
day, others postpone to the next one. Getting it wrong costs a fine.

This module adds that direction to the payment term, so the due date
already comes out right instead of being corrected by hand on every
entry.

The calendar comes from ``l10n_br_resource``, which stacks country,
state and municipality holidays, so a municipal decree that closes the
banks in one city is enough to move an ISS due date there and nowhere
else.

Configuration
=============

1. Load the holiday calendars in *Settings > Technical > Resource >
   Working Schedules*. ``l10n_br_resource`` imports the national ones;
   state and municipal decrees are added as child calendars.
2. On each payment term, choose the *Business day* direction and the
   calendar it follows.

One payment term per tax is the usual setup, because the direction is a
property of the tax, not of the supplier: *DARF anticipates*, *GARE ICMS
postpones*, and so on.

Usage
=====

Nothing changes in the day-to-day: the due date computed on the invoice
already lands on a banking business day.

The shift is applied after the standard computation, so any other module
that changes the due date keeps working, and a term left as *No shift*
behaves exactly as before.
